## Taste calls

**The tiebreak input.** The hard requirement was a real two-tier conflict on one claim rather than a project note wearing client words. So the claim is "who sets the Fieldnote cutover date, and why," and its warrant *is* the org fact: Dermot Sayer reports to the COO, not the CIO, the intake function has never sat under IT, and release management holds a veto rather than the pen. That fires the org-chart/reporting-line row cleanly — a careless reader lands on client. The same claim carries an unmistakable project binding: cutover 2026-01-12, SOW milestone 6 rewritten as change note 4, an off-train controlled change, and an FY27 benefits argument. Signals conflict, so narrowest wins, and the token is `project`.

I kept Dermot out of `pursuits/underwriting-assist/notes/` on purpose. If he appeared in both project directories the recurrence row would fire too, and the input would then be arguing about which of two client signals it carries instead of testing the tiebreak. Verified: he greps to one project directory only.

**Leaning on recurrence, and paying for it.** Nadia Okonjo and the data-sharing review board are both named substantively — quotes, `entities:` frontmatter, open-question resolvers — in notes under both project directories. `grep -l -r` over `{pursuits,projects}/*/notes/` returns two distinct directories for each. That is the one row not readable off the input, so the fixture holds it rather than implying it.

**No record pre-empts an input.** The three planted records (Kestrel's extract window at client scope, Fieldnote's header source at claims-intake, Lantern's scoring basis at the pursuit) are all inert against the four inputs: none is contradicted, and none already states what an input proposes. A planted contradiction would have dragged phase 2.5 into a scenario that is about scope, not conflict.

**Token check by hand, not by lint.** Extracted tokens the linter's way — skip `CLAUDE.md`/`README.md`, take only what `extract_body` yields, strip `[[...]]` first, then read brackets — and matched what survived against the ten-row table including enumerated values. What ships: `[memory candidate: project]` ×2, `[open question: ...]` ×6 with all three fields, `[open question resolved: ...]` ×3 with `resolved:`, `[tension: resolved]` ×2 with `stakes:` and `resolved:`, `[decision: two-way]` ×1 with `committed:` and `discarded:`. No `[memory candidate: journal]` — the trap the excerpt names — and no journal candidate token at all, since input 3's expected outcome is `[journal candidate: <pattern>]` emitted by the run, not planted. The four `_inbox/` inputs contain no square brackets whatsoever, so nothing there can be misread as a token.

**Wiki links stay inside their scope.** `check_links` roots its `find` at the scope under lint, so a project note linking to a client-root record would fail even though `SKILL.md` says cross-scope references are fine. Rather than trip that wart I kept body links intra-scope and used `related: [extends::rWFFzufjXj]` in frontmatter for the one cross-scope relationship worth recording.

**Raw Content.** Matched the sibling fixtures: anchors quote 4-6 words and the zone says the transcript is omitted and the anchors are illustrative. Keeps the files small and byte-stable for the shasum snapshot.
